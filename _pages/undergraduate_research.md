Undergraduate research mentorship is an important part of my
academic work. I am committed to providing students with
opportunities to explore mathematical problems, develop
independent research skills, and communicate their results.

At the University of Michigan, I have mentored undergraduate
students through the **Laboratory of Geometry at Michigan (LoG(M))**,
**Research Experiences for Undergraduates (REU)**, and
**independent studies**. Several of these projects have resulted
in research manuscripts and publications.

## Laboratory of Geometry at Michigan (LoG(M))

From 2024 to 2026, I served as director of the
**Laboratory of Geometry at Michigan (LoG(M))** at the
University of Michigan, an undergraduate research program
that introduces students to mathematical research through
collaborative projects.

As director, I coordinated research activities, recruited
faculty and graduate student mentors, participated in the
selection of undergraduate researchers, and organized
research presentations and outreach activities.

In addition to my administrative responsibilities, I taught 
Math 440 (Geometry Lab), a course focused on developing 
students' mathematical communication skills. Through this course, 
I guided students in presenting their research findings through 
written reports, oral presentations, and posters, helping them 
communicate mathematical ideas clearly and effectively.

## Research Experiences for Undergraduates (REU)

I served as a research mentor in the **Research Experiences
for Undergraduates (REU)** program at the University of
Michigan during the summers of 2024 and 2025.

Through these projects, I guided undergraduate students
in investigating open problems in sub-Riemannian geometry
and geometric control theory. Both projects resulted in
research manuscripts.

### Summer 2025: Metric Lines in the Space of Curves

**Students:** Daniela Catalá and Miriam Vollmayr-Lee.

This project investigated the classification of globally minimizing 
geodesics in the space of curves, using the sequence method 
to establish their global minimality.

The project resulted in the following preprint:

[**Metric Lines in the Space of Curves**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21065)

### Summer 2024: Metric Lines in the Special Euclidean Group

**Students:** Yu Wang and Sean Ku.

This project investigated globally minimizing geodesics
in the special Euclidean group of the plane, SE(2),
a fundamental example in sub-Riemannian geometry and
geometric control theory.

The resulting paper was published in *Involve* in 2026:

[**Metric Lines in the Special Euclidean Group on the Plane**](https://doi.org/10.2140/involve.2026.19.471)


## Independent Studies

I have supervised independent study projects that allow
undergraduate students to explore advanced mathematical
topics and develop their own research questions.

These projects emphasize mathematical reasoning,
independent problem-solving, and scientific communication.
Several have resulted in research manuscripts.

### Marcus T. C. Lau — Optimal Synthesis on the Special Linear Group

This project investigated the Riemannian geometry of
the special linear group $\mathbb{SL}_2(\mathbb{R})$,
focusing on the determination of cut times, the cut locus,
and the injectivity radius.

The project resulted in a research manuscript submitted
to *Differential Geometry and its Applications*.

**Research manuscript::**

[Optimal Synthesis on the Special Linear Group](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16222)


### Yoshihiro Paris — Irrationality of Infinite Products

This independent study originated from Yoshihiro's interest
in irrational numbers and number theory. During the Winter
2025 semester, he approached me with ideas concerning the
irrationality of infinite products. His enthusiasm and 
persistence inspired me to supervise this project, 
which provided an opportunity to support undergraduate 
research beyond my primary area of expertise.

The project investigated the irrationality of infinite
products of the form

$$
\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{r}{A^n}\right),
$$

where $A\in\mathbb{Z}$ and $r\in\mathbb{Q}$ satisfy certain
technical conditions.

Yoshihiro developed an elementary proof of irrationality
for this family of products. Although more general results
were previously established by Bundschuh in 1969, his
approach provides an accessible alternative using techniques
suitable for undergraduate students.

My role was to help Yoshihiro formalize his arguments,
strengthen the mathematical rigor of his proof, and
develop the manuscript into a research paper.

[**Read the manuscript (PDF)**]({{ '/assets/pdf/Paris.pdf' | relative_url }})
*The Irrationality of $\;0.8\cdot 0.98\cdot 0.998\cdot
0.9998\cdots$*, submitted to the
*Rose-Hulman Undergraduate Mathematics Journal*.
