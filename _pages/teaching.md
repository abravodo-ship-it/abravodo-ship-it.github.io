I have over twelve years of experience teaching mathematics
at the university level, including courses at the
University of Michigan, the University of California,
Santa Cruz, and the National Autonomous University of
Mexico (UNAM).

My teaching emphasizes mathematical reasoning,
problem-solving, and communication. I aim to create
an inclusive learning environment where students feel
comfortable asking questions, exploring different
approaches, and developing confidence in their
mathematical abilities.

In 2025, I received the **B. Alan Taylor Outstanding
Postdoctoral Assistant Professor Teaching Award**
from the University of Michigan.

## Teaching Philosophy

My primary goal as a mathematics educator is to help students 
become independent mathematical thinkers. I seek to develop their 
confidence in approaching unfamiliar problems, constructing 
mathematical arguments, and communicating their ideas clearly. 
This goal guides my course design, classroom teaching, office hours, 
and mentorship.

I pursue this goal through three complementary strategies:
creating a supportive learning environment, designing
problems that encourage multiple approaches, and helping
students recognize connections between mathematical ideas.

#### Creating a Supportive Learning Environment

I believe that students learn mathematics more effectively
when they feel comfortable asking questions, making mistakes,
and discussing their ideas. I emphasize that seeking help
is an essential part of learning and that both the classroom
and office hours are spaces where students' questions
are welcome.

I also acknowledge my own mistakes and encourage students
to make conjectures during class discussions. My goal is
to help students recognize that mathematical reasoning
develops through exploration, practice, and feedback.

This approach was strongly influenced by my experience
as a literacy educator in Mexico. For three years,
I participated in the
[Campaña de Alfabetización Cemac–Logos–C.I.E.](http://adeco.org.mx/programas/alfabetiza/),
teaching adults in Indigenous communities.

The program introduced me to Paulo Freire's educational
philosophy and his culture-circle method, which emphasizes
dialogue and the experiences of learners. I learned the
importance of creating an environment in which students'
ideas are valued and their experiences become part of
the learning process.

#### Designing Problems to Support Mathematical Understanding

In my courses, I design activities that encourage students
to explore different approaches, collaborate with their
peers, and explain their mathematical reasoning.

For example, in **Math 116: Integral Calculus** at the
University of Michigan, I used team-based seating and
collaborative worksheets to encourage students to solve
problems together.

While teaching the ratio test for series convergence,
I noticed that some students were unfamiliar with
factorial expressions. I therefore designed an activity
that broke a complicated factorial quotient into
intermediate steps before asking students to determine
the radius of convergence.

This experience illustrates an important aspect of my
teaching: adapting mathematical problems to students'
different levels of preparation without sacrificing
conceptual understanding.

I also emphasize mathematical communication. In
**Math 440: Laboratory of Geometry**, I worked with
undergraduate students on mathematical writing, oral
presentations, and research posters.

For example, I introduced students to the structure
of mathematical definitions and asked each research
team to apply these principles to a central definition
from its own project.

#### Connecting Mathematical Ideas

I aim to present mathematics as a cohesive body of
knowledge rather than a collection of isolated topics.
I design problems that connect ideas from previous
courses to more advanced mathematics and applications
in other disciplines.

At UNAM, while teaching students from different
academic programs, I used linear algebra to introduce
the mathematical formulation of the Hardy–Weinberg
principle and its connections to genetics and evolution.

At the University of Michigan, in **Math 316: Ordinary
Differential Equations with Proofs**, I emphasized
connections between differential equations and
linear algebra.

For example, I introduced linear differential operators 
and guided students to understand the orthogonality of 
Legendre polynomials through their interpretation as eigenfunctions 
of self-adjoint Sturm–Liouville operators.

Through these connections, I hope students develop
a deeper understanding of mathematics and recognize
how ideas learned in one course can become powerful
tools in another.

#### Undergraduate Research and Mathematical Independence

My teaching philosophy also guides my approach to
undergraduate research. I seek open problems that
students can investigate using mathematics they
already know, while introducing them to broader
research questions and more advanced mathematical
ideas.

Through this process, students develop independence,
creativity, and confidence in their mathematical abilities.
