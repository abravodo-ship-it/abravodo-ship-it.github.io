My research lies at the intersection of **sub-Riemannian geometry,
geometric mechanics, and geometric control theory**. My main interests
 include the classification of globally minimizing geodesics, 
 the geometry and dynamics of geodesic flows on Lie groups, 
 the integrability of Hamiltonian systems, and the global dynamics of 
 nonholonomic mechanical systems, particularly the transition 
 from integrable to chaotic behavior.

## Metric Lines and the Sequence Method

One of my main research interests is the classification of
**metric lines**, namely, geodesics that minimize distance globally
between any two of their points. Understanding these curves is a
fundamental problem in sub-Riemannian geometry, where the presence
of nonholonomic constraints makes the global behavior of geodesics
particularly challenging to analyze.

The state of the art in establishing the global minimality of 
sub-Riemannian geodesics has traditionally involved techniques 
such as optimal synthesis and Hamilton–Jacobi theory. These 
approaches often require explicit integration of the geodesic 
equations or the construction of suitable calibrations. 
In contrast, I developed the **sequence method**, which establishes 
global minimality through compactness arguments and the geometric 
properties of minimizing sequences.


I have applied the sequence method to investigate metric lines 
in several Carnot groups, including jet spaces and Engel-type groups. 
These applications illustrate how the method can be used 
to study global minimality without explicitly integrating the geodesic equations.


## Integrability and Reduction of Geodesic Flows

Another direction of my research focuses on the integrability 
of sub-Riemannian geodesic flows on Lie groups and the use of 
reduction techniques to investigate their dynamics.

In joint work with P. Arathoon and A. M. Bloch, 
we established the **Liouville integrability** of a sub-Riemannian 
geodesic flow on the orthogonal group using Poisson reduction. 
A key ingredient of our approach is the construction of first 
integrals for the sub-Riemannian flow by taking suitable limits 
of first integrals of the corresponding Riemannian geodesic flows.

In a related project with E. Le Donne and N. Paddeu, we developed 
a framework for the **symplectic reduction of sub-Riemannian geodesic
 flows on metabelian nilpotent Lie groups**, using normal abelian 
 subgroups. As an application of this framework, we established 
 the Liouville integrability of the sub-Riemannian geodesic flow 
 on Engel-type Carnot groups.

## Nonholonomic Mechanics and Billiards

My research in geometric mechanics focuses on nonholonomic systems,
 whose motion is subject to non-integrable velocity constraints. 
 A central goal of my research is to understand their global dynamics, 
 particularly the mechanisms underlying the transition from 
 integrable to chaotic behavior.

In joint work with A. M. Bloch and W. Clark, we established 
the integrability of a knife-edge billiard moving inside 
a circular domain. This system combines nonholonomic 
motion with elastic impacts at the boundary. Our analysis 
reveals the geometric structure of the impact dynamics and 
establishes the existence of a family of generalized caustics.

In earlier joint work with L. C. García-Naranjo, we investigated 
the dynamics of an articulated **n-trailer vehicle**, a classical 
nonholonomic mechanical system, focusing on the qualitative 
behavior of its trajectories.

## Optimal Synthesis and Geometric Control

Another direction of my research focuses on the application of geometric optimal 
control methods to determine cut times, cut loci, and globally minimizing geodesics.

Together with my undergraduate student Marcus T. C. Lau, we investigated 
the Riemannian geometry of the special linear group 
$\mathbb{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$. We obtained a geometric characterization of 
the cut locus, showing that it consists of symmetric negative-definite 
matrices. We also derived an explicit formula for the injectivity radius.

These results contribute to understanding the global
geometry of Riemannian geodesics on Lie groups.
